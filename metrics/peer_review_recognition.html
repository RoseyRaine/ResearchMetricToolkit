Peer review recognition

Note: this page was previously titled "Publons score". Publons was retired and merged into Web of Science in August 2022, and the specific 1–10 quality/significance article score it used to display is no longer an active feature — Clarivate has confirmed this scoring was discontinued in the migration. This page has been rewritten to reflect the two sources CSU researchers can currently use to evidence peer review contributions.

Field Field Value
Name Peer review recognition
Can Apply To An individual researcher's record of peer review and editorial contributions to journals, conferences, and funders.
Metric Definition A verified count and history of a researcher's peer review activity — the number of reviews completed, for which journals, and (where visible) editorial board or handling-editor roles. This is not a citation-based metric but a record of scholarly service, used as evidence of a researcher's standing and contribution to their field beyond their own publications.
Metric Calculation Reviews are typically verified either automatically (via publisher/editorial system integration) or by forwarding a review confirmation email to the platform. Verified reviews are then totalled and can be broken down by journal, by year, or as a peer-review-to-publication ratio. No quality or significance score is calculated from the review content itself.
Data Sources Web of Science Researcher Profile (formerly Publons, merged into Web of Science in 2022) — verified peer review counts, editorial board memberships, and a peer-review-to-publication ratio, viewable on a researcher's public profile. ORCID — peer review activity can also be recorded on a researcher's ORCID record, either manually or via integrations with participating publishers and review platforms, giving a second, publisher-independent record.
Appropriate Use Cases Useful in promotion, tenure, and grant applications as evidence of standing in a research community and service to the discipline, particularly where a CV section calls for evidence of editorial or reviewing contributions. Having records on both Web of Science and ORCID is worthwhile, since the two draw on different verification pathways and together give a more complete picture than either alone.
Limitations Coverage depends on the researcher actively registering reviews (or their journal automatically verifying them), so an incomplete profile understates actual reviewing activity rather than indicating a lack of it. Neither Web of Science Researcher Profile nor ORCID assesses the quality of a review's content, only that a review took place.
Inappropriate Use Cases Should not be used as a proxy for the quality or rigour of a researcher's reviewing, only the volume and verification of their reviewing activity. Should not be compared directly between researchers in different fields, where review load and journal culture vary considerably.
Available Metric Sources Web of Science Researcher Profile (Clarivate); ORCID.
Transparency Both platforms rely on self-registration or publisher-confirmed verification, and reviewer anonymity settings (where a journal's policy requires it) are respected, so review counts may be visible even where the specific journal or manuscript is not.
Website Web of Science Researcher Profile, ORCID
Timeframe Reflects reviews completed since the researcher began recording them on either platform; historical Publons reviews prior to 2022 were carried over in the migration to Web of Science.
