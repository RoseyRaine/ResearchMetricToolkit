Source Normalized Impact per Paper (SNIP)

Field Field Value
Name Source Normalized Impact per Paper (SNIP)
Can Apply To Journals and other serial titles indexed in Scopus.
Metric Definition SNIP measures a journal's citation impact while correcting for differences in citation practices between subject fields. It is calculated by CWTS (Centre for Science and Technology Studies, Leiden University) using Scopus data, and is one of the field-normalized journal metrics available in Scopus alongside CiteScore and SJR.
Metric Calculation SNIP is the ratio of a journal's citations per publication (its "raw impact per paper") to the citation potential of its subject field — the average number of citations papers in that field typically receive. Dividing by the field's citation potential normalizes for the fact that some fields cite more frequently, and over longer citation windows, than others. A SNIP of 1.0 indicates a journal's citation impact is exactly average for its field; above 1.0 indicates higher-than-average impact relative to the field.
Data Sources Scopus (Elsevier) publication and citation data, with the SNIP calculation methodology developed and maintained by CWTS at Leiden University.
Appropriate Use Cases Because it is field-normalized, SNIP is more suitable than CiteScore or the Journal Impact Factor for comparing journals across different subject areas, since it corrects for varying citation density and publication practices between fields. Useful when comparing potential journals to submit to across disciplines.
Limitations The field-normalization relies on the subject classification system used (Scopus's All Science Journal Classification), which can be a source of inconsistency if a journal is miscategorised or spans multiple fields. As with other journal-level metrics, SNIP reflects the average performance of the journal, not any individual article within it.
Inappropriate Use Cases Should not be used to evaluate an individual article or author. Should not be treated as a direct measure of research quality — like all citation-based metrics, it reflects citation attention, not the intrinsic merit of the work.
Available Metric Sources Scopus (Elsevier); SNIP values and methodology are also published via the CWTS Journal Indicators portal.
Transparency The calculation methodology is published by CWTS and is independently maintained from Elsevier, which is regarded as a strength for transparency. SNIP values are freely accessible via the CWTS Journal Indicators website without a Scopus subscription.
Website CWTS Journal Indicators
Timeframe Calculated annually, typically using a three-year citation window; historical values are available via the CWTS Journal Indicators portal.
