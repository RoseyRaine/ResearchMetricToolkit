CiteScore

Field Field Value
Name CiteScore
Can Apply To Journals and other serial titles (e.g. book series, conference proceedings) indexed in Scopus.
Metric Definition CiteScore is a journal-level metric that measures the average number of citations received per document published in a serial title over a defined period. It is Scopus's equivalent to the Journal Impact Factor, and is calculated and made freely available by Elsevier for every active title indexed in Scopus.
Metric Calculation A journal's CiteScore for year X is the total citations received in year X by documents published in that journal in years X−1, X−2 and X−3, divided by the total number of documents published in that journal in those same three years. Since 2020, the calculation includes only peer-reviewed document types: articles, reviews, conference papers, data papers and book chapters.
Data Sources Scopus (Elsevier), which indexes a broader set of journals than the Journal Citation Reports used for the Journal Impact Factor.
Appropriate Use Cases Useful for comparing the citation performance of journals within the same subject field, and for identifying journals with a wide readership when choosing where to submit a manuscript. CiteScore is freely available (unlike the subscription-based Journal Impact Factor), making it a more accessible option for authors without institutional access to Journal Citation Reports.
Limitations Like the Journal Impact Factor, CiteScore is not field-weighted, so raw scores cannot be meaningfully compared across disciplines with different citation and publishing norms. It reflects the average citation performance of a journal, not the citation performance of any individual article published in it, and averages can be skewed by a small number of highly cited papers.
Inappropriate Use Cases Should not be used to evaluate an individual article or author — a journal-level average says nothing about the citation performance of a specific paper. Should not be compared across subject areas without adjustment, since citation practices and publication volumes vary widely by field.
Available Metric Sources Scopus and the CiteScore metrics pages on Scopus.com, both maintained by Elsevier.
Transparency The calculation method is publicly documented by Elsevier and CiteScore values are freely accessible without a Scopus subscription, which is more transparent than the subscription-gated Journal Impact Factor.
Website Scopus Sources (CiteScore)
Timeframe Calculated annually using a rolling three-year citation window, with historical CiteScore values available back to 2011.
