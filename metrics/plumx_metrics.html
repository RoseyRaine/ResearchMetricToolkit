PlumX Metrics (overview)

PlumX Metrics is a suite of article-level metrics owned by Elsevier and surfaced throughout Scopus and other platforms as a "Plum Print" — a set of coloured rings summarising attention to a single research output. Several individual metrics in this Toolkit (for example Mendeley Readers and Github: Forks, collaborators, watchers) are themselves PlumX-sourced data points. This page gives an overview of the category as a whole; see the individual metric pages for details on each component.

Field Field Value
Name PlumX Metrics
Can Apply To Individual research outputs, including articles, conference proceedings, book chapters, datasets, and software.
Metric Definition PlumX Metrics group online attention to a research output into five categories: Citations (traditional citation counts alongside societal citation types such as clinical, patent, or policy citations), Usage (e.g. downloads, views, holdings), Captures (e.g. bookmarks, saves in reference managers such as Mendeley), Mentions (e.g. blog posts, news stories, reviews), and Social Media (e.g. mentions on platforms such as X/Twitter). Rather than producing a single combined score, PlumX presents the underlying counts for each category, visualised as the Plum Print.
Metric Calculation Each of the five categories is a simple count of the relevant activity captured from a defined list of sources (e.g. Mendeley for Captures, specific news and blog outlets for Mentions). Unlike the Altmetric Attention Score, PlumX does not apply a weighted formula to produce one aggregate number; the categories are reported and visualised separately.
Data Sources PlumX aggregates data from a wide range of sources depending on the category, including Mendeley, Scopus citation data, PubMed Central, patent databases, policy document trackers, news outlets, and social media platforms.
Appropriate Use Cases Useful for building a broader, more immediate picture of engagement with a research output than citations alone provide, particularly for outputs that are too recent to have accumulated citations, or for output types (datasets, software) that are not well served by traditional citation metrics. Because PlumX separates its categories, it can support a more nuanced narrative than a single combined altmetric score — for example distinguishing scholarly capture (Mendeley saves) from public attention (news mentions).
Limitations Coverage varies by category and can be inconsistent, particularly for older outputs or those published outside major indexing sources. As with other altmetrics, high counts can reflect volume of attention rather than quality, and some categories (e.g. Social Media) are especially susceptible to manipulation or one-off viral attention unrelated to research merit.
Inappropriate Use Cases Should not be used as a substitute for citation-based or peer-review-based assessment of research quality. Raw counts should not be compared across disciplines or output types without context, since baseline levels of online attention vary considerably.
Available Metric Sources PlumX Metrics are available via Scopus and Elsevier's institutional PlumX Dashboards product.
Transparency Elsevier publishes a general description of the five categories and the types of sources feeding each one, but the specific source lists and any weighting or de-duplication logic are not fully published.
Website Plum Analytics: About the Metrics
Timeframe Metrics accumulate from the point of publication (or online availability) onward and are updated on an ongoing basis as new attention data is captured.
