Patent citations

Field Field Value
Name Patent citations
Can Apply To Individual research outputs (most commonly journal articles), an author's body of work, or an institution's overall output.
Metric Definition A count of instances where a research output is cited in the "prior art" or references section of a granted patent or patent application, indicating that the research informed or was consulted during the development of a patented invention. This is treated both as an output-level metric (a specific paper's patent citations) and as a broader indicator of a research output's applied, technological, or commercial relevance.
Metric Calculation A simple count of the number of patents citing a given research output, or the number of a researcher's outputs cited across all patents. Some sources also report the number of distinct patent families (grouping related patent filings across jurisdictions for the same invention) rather than raw patent document counts, which avoids inflating the count when one invention is filed in multiple countries.
Data Sources PlumX Metrics and Altmetric both include patent citation data, drawing on patent databases including the Lens (Cambia), PATSTAT, and national/regional patent offices (e.g. USPTO, EPO). Dimensions (Digital Science) also links publications to citing patents.
Appropriate Use Cases Useful evidence of applied or translational impact, particularly for STEM disciplines where research may feed into industry innovation. Relevant in grant applications, industry engagement narratives, and impact case studies where demonstrating a pathway from research to technological application is valued.
Limitations Patent citation practices vary significantly by country, patent office, and technology sector, and citing conventions differ between patent examiners (who add citations during examination) and applicants (who cite prior art they are aware of), so counts are not directly comparable across jurisdictions. A patent citation does not indicate whether the research was central or peripheral to the invention. Coverage also depends on the tracking source, and smaller or non-English-language patent offices may be under-represented.
Inappropriate Use Cases Should not be used as a measure of research quality on its own, nor assumed to reflect commercial value or the significance of the invention it contributed to. Should not be compared across disciplines, since patent citation is common in some fields (e.g. chemistry, engineering, materials science) and rare or near non-existent in others (e.g. humanities, most social sciences).
Available Metric Sources PlumX Metrics, Altmetric, Dimensions, and the Lens (Cambia) all provide patent citation tracking linked to scholarly outputs.
Transparency Granted patents and their citations are public documents and independently verifiable via national patent office databases; however, tracking tools vary in how comprehensively they match patent citations back to the correct scholarly output, so a "zero" count may reflect a matching gap rather than a genuine absence of citation.
Website The Lens
Timeframe Patent citations can appear at any point after a research output's publication, often with a lag of several years given the time typically required for patent examination and grant.
