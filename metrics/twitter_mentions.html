X (formerly Twitter) mentions

Field Field Value
Name X (formerly Twitter) Mentions
Can Apply To Any scholarly product (e.g. journal article) shared on X (formerly Twitter) using a link
Metric Definition X mentions include posts and reposts (formerly "tweets" and "retweets") that reference a trackable scholarly product. Twitter was rebranded to X in 2023; altmetric providers and the platform itself now use "posts" and "reposts", though most tracking tools and much of the literature still refer to this data as "Twitter mentions" or "tweets" for consistency with historical data.
Metric Calculation Counts are determined by the number of registered Twitter users that tweet or retweet a post that links to a trackable scholarly product. Depending on the metric data source, mentions may be parsed and presented in different ways. This information can include registered Twitter users’ activity, user demographic information, and user network information, such as follower counts and handles. Note that contrary to popular belief, research outputs do not have to be tweeted using DOI links (e.g. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41590-017-0014-x) in order to be tracked by altmetrics services–nearly all services now are able to identify tweeted scholarly outputs in other ways.
Data Sources X (formerly Twitter)
Appropriate Use Cases Twitter citation counts and contextual demographic and network information can be used to identify the sharing of and reach of information about research on social media, especially within the first weeks of publication (Priem & Costello, 2010). Information about who is tweeting and the nature of the tweets is needed to demonstrate the kind and quality of engagement, such as uptake by a scientific audience or interest from a public audience (Thelwall & Kousha, 2015).
Limitations Tweet counts can be gamed and there are differences in Twitter use to discuss research across disciplines. Like other metrics, a count of tweets does not reveal the kind of impact an article or other scholarly product had (Thelwall, Tsou, Weingart, Holmberg & Haustein, 2013).
Inappropriate Use Cases X citation counts should not be used as a direct measure for a specific kind of impact, such as public engagement.
Available Metric Sources Twitter.com and the Twitter API for recent Twitter activity; Altmetric, PlumX, Impactstory, and Crossref Event Data for historical Twitter activity.
Transparency The transparency of X mentions depends on the source. Some sources are completely auditable in that you can see counts, read all posts, and access user demographic and network information, though API access changes since 2023 have made some data harder for third-party tools to retrieve than in Twitter’s earlier years.
Website Twitter.com
Timeframe Twitter was launched in March 2006 and rebranded to X in 2023. Altmetric, PlumX, and Impactstory began tracking the service in earnest in 2011, meaning that a majority of the historical data on those sites includes scholarly products created in 2011 and beyond.
