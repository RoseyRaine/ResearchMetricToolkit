Citations, Conference papers

Field Field Value
Name Citations, Conference papers
Can Apply To Individual conference papers, proceedings papers, and extended abstracts, as well as an author's or institution's body of conference output.
Metric Definition A count of the number of times a conference paper has been cited in later scholarly work. Conference papers are a particularly important output type in fields such as computer science, engineering, and some areas of the physical sciences, where peer-reviewed conference proceedings are often a primary (rather than secondary) publication venue, unlike most other disciplines where journal articles predominate.
Metric Calculation A simple count of citing documents that reference the conference paper, drawn from the citation indexing of the database used. Some databases calculate a Field Weighted Citation Impact or similar normalized measure for conference papers, though normalization is less reliable for this output type given lower and more variable indexing coverage compared to journal articles.
Data Sources Scopus and Web of Science both index conference proceedings, though coverage varies significantly by discipline and by publisher (e.g. IEEE and ACM proceedings are well indexed; many smaller or regional conferences are not). Google Scholar generally has the broadest coverage of conference literature, since it also indexes preprint and open repository versions of proceedings papers.
Appropriate Use Cases Appropriate for demonstrating impact in disciplines where conference proceedings are a primary publication venue and citation vehicle, most notably computer science and some engineering fields. Useful alongside journal citation counts when a researcher's output spans both venue types, to give a complete picture of their citation impact.
Limitations Indexing coverage of conference proceedings is markedly less comprehensive and more inconsistent than for journal articles, so citation counts for conference papers are more likely to understate true impact due to missing citing or cited documents. Citation norms and typical citation counts for conference papers vary enormously by field, making cross-disciplinary comparison particularly unreliable for this output type.
Inappropriate Use Cases Should not be compared directly against journal article citation counts without accounting for the different citation and indexing norms between the two output types. Should not be used to judge researchers in disciplines where conference papers are a minor or preliminary output type rather than the primary venue for disseminating results.
Available Metric Sources Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar.
Transparency Citation counts are generally auditable back to citing documents, but coverage gaps (particularly for smaller or non-English-language conferences) mean an apparently low count should be checked against more than one source before being treated as definitive.
Website n/a
Timeframe Citation accrual for conference papers can be faster than for journal articles in fast-moving fields such as computer science, where conference proceedings are often the first point of publication and citation.
