SCImago Journal Rank (SJR) and quartiles

This is a journal-quality metric, not a peer review record — see Peer review recognition if you're looking for evidence of reviewing activity instead.

Field Field Value
Name SCImago Journal Rank (SJR) and quartiles
Can Apply To Journals and other serial titles indexed in Scopus.
Metric Definition SJR is a prestige-weighted journal-level metric, based on the idea that not all citations are equal — a citation from a highly-cited journal is worth more than one from a rarely-cited journal, similar in principle to Google's PageRank algorithm. Journals within each Scopus subject category are also ranked and divided into four quartiles (Q1–Q4) based on their SJR, with Q1 representing the top 25% of journals in that category and Q4 the bottom 25%. Quartile ranking is the figure most commonly quoted by researchers and used in promotion cases.
Metric Calculation SJR uses an iterative algorithm (based on eigenvector centrality) that weights each citation by the prestige of the citing journal, calculated over a three-year citation window. Once every journal in a subject category has an SJR value, the journals are ranked from highest to lowest and split into four equal quartiles (Q1–Q4). A journal that is cross-listed in more than one subject category can hold a different quartile in each.
Data Sources Scopus (Elsevier) publication and citation data, with SJR calculated and published by SCImago Lab (a research group based in Spain, independent of Elsevier).
Appropriate Use Cases Widely used by researchers when choosing where to submit a manuscript, and by promotion and grant committees as one indicator of journal standing. Because SJR is prestige-weighted rather than a simple citation average, it is often considered a useful complement to CiteScore and the Journal Impact Factor rather than a direct substitute for either.
Limitations Quartile boundaries depend on the size and citation density of the subject category a journal is placed in, so a Q1 ranking in a small or low-citing field is not directly comparable to a Q1 ranking in a large, highly-cited field. Journals are sometimes categorised into subject areas that only partially match their actual scope, which can distort their quartile placement.
Inappropriate Use Cases Should not be used to evaluate an individual article or author — like other journal-level metrics, it reflects the standing of the journal, not the specific paper published in it. Quartile rank should not be compared across different subject categories without accounting for how each category's journal population and citation norms differ.
Available Metric Sources The free SCImago Journal & Country Rank portal (scimagojr.com); SJR and quartile data are also integrated into Scopus journal listings.
Transparency SCImago publishes its calculation methodology openly, and SJR values and quartile rankings are freely accessible without a Scopus subscription, unlike the subscription-gated Journal Impact Factor.
Website SCImago Journal & Country Rank
Timeframe Calculated and published annually, with historical SJR values and quartile rankings available on the SCImago portal back to 1999.
