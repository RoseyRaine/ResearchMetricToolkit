Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) mapping

Field Field Value
Name Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) mapping
Can Apply To Individual publications, an author's body of work, a research group, or an institution's overall output.
Metric Definition SDG mapping links research outputs to one or more of the 17 Sustainable Development Goals defined by the United Nations (e.g. SDG 3: Good health and wellbeing, SDG 4: Quality education, SDG 11: Sustainable cities and communities). It is not a citation-based metric but a topical classification: a publication is identified as contributing evidence, data, or discussion relevant to a given goal. Once mapped, publications can be grouped by SDG and analysed using other metrics, such as volume of output or average Field Weighted Citation Impact (FWCI), for that goal.
Metric Calculation SDG mapping is not a mathematical calculation but a classification exercise. Providers such as Elsevier (via SciVal/Scopus) and Clarivate (via InCites/Web of Science) use a combination of query-based searches (keywords, subject terms) and machine-learning classification, refined through manual review by subject experts, to assign publications to one or more SDGs. A single publication can be mapped to multiple goals. Once mapped, aggregate figures (e.g. number of publications per SDG, average FWCI per SDG) can be produced for an author, group, or institution.
Data Sources SciVal and Scopus (Elsevier's SDG Research Mapping initiative) and InCites/Web of Science (Clarivate's SDG mapping) are the two main sources used by universities. Both rely on the underlying citation database's indexing (Scopus or Web of Science) and therefore inherit the coverage limitations of those databases.
Appropriate Use Cases Useful for demonstrating the societal relevance and reach of research, particularly in grant applications, promotion cases, and institutional reporting where alignment with global priorities is valued. It can help identify potential collaborators working on the same goal, and is used by ranking systems such as the Times Higher Education Impact Rankings to evidence an institution's contribution to the SDGs.
Limitations Mapping methodologies differ between providers (query-based vs. machine-learning classification), so the same publication may be mapped to different SDGs, or a different number of SDGs, depending on the source used. Mapping is applied at the publication level and can miss nuance: a paper may touch on a goal without being primarily "about" it. Coverage is also limited to what is indexed in the underlying citation database, so outputs in databases with less comprehensive coverage of a discipline (e.g. some humanities or creative outputs) may be under-represented.
Inappropriate Use Cases SDG mapping should not be used as a measure of research quality or impact on its own — it indicates topical relevance, not citation performance or societal outcome. It should not be used to rank researchers or institutions against one another without accounting for differing disciplinary mixes, since some fields naturally map to SDGs more readily than others.
Available Metric Sources SciVal and Scopus (Elsevier), InCites and Web of Science (Clarivate), and Dimensions (Digital Science) all provide some form of SDG classification and reporting.
Transparency Elsevier and Clarivate publish high-level descriptions of their mapping methodologies (search queries and/or classification models, refined by expert review), but the full underlying query sets and models are proprietary and not independently auditable.
Website Elsevier SDG Research Mapping
Timeframe SDG mapping can be applied retrospectively across an author's or institution's full publication history, and is typically updated as new publications are indexed.
